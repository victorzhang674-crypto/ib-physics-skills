# Physics IA rubric and scoring

Coaching summary of the four criteria in the IB *Physics guide* (updated November 2024), printed pp. 67–75. Use the official descriptors and clarifications if supplied. Each criterion is worth 6 marks and the total is 24.

| Criterion | 1–2 | 3–4 | 5–6 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research design | Gives the question without context, mentions choices about collecting relevant data, and leaves gaps that prevent reproduction. | Places the question in a broad context, describes choices for getting enough relevant data, and gives a method that can be followed with minor gaps. | Grounds the question in the specific system and relevant physics, explains why the choices yield enough useful data, and describes a reproducible procedure or selection method. |
| Data analysis | Recording and processing are hard to follow and imprecise. Uncertainty receives little attention, and major processing issues undermine a valid answer. | Recording and processing are clear or precise, with some uncertainty treatment. Substantial gaps or errors limit the detail or validity of a possible answer. | Recording and processing are both clear and precise. Uncertainties are treated appropriately, and the analysis is accurate and relevant to the question. |
| Conclusion | Gives a relevant answer that is unsupported by the analysis and a superficial comparison to accepted science. | Develops a relevant answer with some comparison to accepted science, though the claim does not fully follow from the analysis. | Justifies a direct answer from the processed results and their uncertainties, and uses a relevant, traceable comparison with accepted science to support the judgment. |
| Evaluation | Mentions generic weaknesses or limitations and states feasible improvements. | Describes specific weaknesses or limitations and describes feasible improvements that address them. | Explains how much the specific issues matter relative to one another and explains why each proposed feasible change would address the linked issue. |

## Apply best fit

1. Identify the question and review the whole submitted report, including tables and figures. Collect concrete evidence for each criterion across sections. Score each criterion on its own and choose the band that best reflects the balance of evidence. Within the band, choose the upper mark when its qualities are demonstrated to a greater extent and the lower mark when evidence is weaker. The top band can include imperfections. Use 0 when the available complete work falls below the first descriptor.
2. Distinguish a confirmed omission in a complete report from evidence unavailable in the supplied draft or unreadable attachment. For incomplete material, identify pending judgments and give provisional marks only where useful. Avoid a definitive total when criteria cannot be assessed.
3. Give a row per criterion with the estimated mark, a page, table or paragraph reference, why the evidence fits that band, and the most consequential improvement. For example, identify whether a processing error prevents a valid conclusion or just limits its precision, then explain which recalculation would address it. In Evaluation, distinguish a design weakness such as friction biasing the slope from a scope limitation such as the studied range. Assess the relative impact and the proposed fix using the student's actual method and results.
4. Sum all four whole marks for a complete assessment and report the estimate out of 24. Translate it into a short revision plan ordered by the effects on the investigation. A component score alone cannot establish the student's final course grade.
5. When asked for draft feedback, also give numbered, location-specific comments like a teacher's annotations (section or paragraph, issue, fix), checked against the [section checklist](checklist.md). Keep each comment actionable.

## Legacy criteria and exemplars

Many school marking grids, guides and exemplars predate 2025 and use five criteria: Personal engagement (2), Exploration (6), Analysis (6), Evaluation (6) and Communication (4). Identify which scheme a supplied document uses and score against the current four criteria. Map legacy feedback as follows:

| Legacy criterion | Current home |
| --- | --- |
| Exploration (question, background, method, factors, safety) | Research design |
| Analysis (raw data, processing, uncertainties) | Data analysis |
| Analysis (interpretation of processed data) and Evaluation (conclusion, scientific context) | Conclusion |
| Evaluation (weaknesses, limitations, improvements, extensions) | Evaluation |
| Personal engagement and Communication | No separate marks. Clear structure, correct conventions and justified independent choices still support every criterion. |

Use legacy exemplars for structure and technique. Do not carry over requirements that no longer apply, such as a stated personal reason for the topic, and do not treat a legacy score as a current mark.
